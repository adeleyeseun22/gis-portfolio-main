## Project Overview

This project demonstrates a QGIS-based remote sensing workflow for analyzing urban built-up intensity and vegetation patterns using Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery. Dubai was selected as the pilot geography because it provides a globally recognizable urban environment with strong contrast between built-up zones, desert surfaces, managed vegetation, and coastal features.

The workflow uses Red, Near Infrared, and Shortwave Infrared bands from Sentinel-2 imagery to derive two remote sensing indices: **NDVI** for vegetation signal and **NDBI** for built-up intensity. These outputs were used to examine urban and environmental patterns and to create index-based change maps between 2019 and 2025.

## Problem Statement

Rapid urban development requires spatial evidence to understand how built-up areas, vegetation, and land-surface patterns are changing over time. This project explores how open satellite imagery and QGIS can be used to produce practical remote-sensing outputs for urban planning, environmental monitoring, infrastructure analysis, and geospatial consulting.

## Data Used

- Sentinel-2 Red band (B04): Used with NIR to calculate NDVI.
- Sentinel-2 Near Infrared band (B08): Used in both NDVI and NDBI calculations.
- SSentinel-2 Shortwave Infrared band (B11): Used with NIR to calculate NDBI after resampling from 20 m to 10 m.
- Sentinel-2 True Colour Image (TCI): Used as a visual satellite reference map.
- 2019 and 2025 Sentinel-2 L2A image products: Used for index-based change comparison across time.


## Methodology

1. Created a dedicated QGIS project for Dubai urban growth and vegetation analysis using **EPSG:32640 — WGS 84 / UTM Zone 40N**.
2. Downloaded Sentinel-2 L2A image products for 2019 and 2025 from Copernicus Browser.
3. Extracted required image bands from the SAFE product folders: **B04, B08, B11, and TCI**.
4. Loaded the Red, Near Infrared, Shortwave Infrared, and True Colour Image bands into QGIS.
5. Resampled the B11 Shortwave Infrared band from 20 m to 10 m to align with the 10 m Red and Near Infrared bands.
6. Calculated NDVI using the Near Infrared and Red bands.
7. Calculated NDBI using the resampled Shortwave Infrared and Near Infrared bands.
8. Generated change rasters by subtracting 2019 index outputs from 2025 index outputs.
9. Styled the raster outputs using pseudocolor and diverging symbology with interpretation labels.
10. Designed and exported professional map layouts for portfolio presentation.

## Analytical Measures

- NDVI: Indicates vegetation signal and vegetation pattern.
- NDBI: Indicates built-up intensity or urban surface signal.
- NDVI Change: Shows vegetation increase, decrease, or relative stability.
- NDBI Change: Shows increase or decrease in built-up signal.

## Portfolio Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates how multispectral satellite imagery can be transformed into practical geospatial outputs for urban and environmental assessment. NDBI provides an exploratory indicator of built-up intensity, while NDVI highlights vegetation distribution and managed green areas. The change maps provide a simplified way to visualize where built-up signal and vegetation signal may have increased or decreased between 2019 and 2025.

This project adds a remote-sensing dimension to the portfolio and complements vector-based service accessibility work. It shows the ability to handle raster data, derive analytical indices, interpret spatial outputs, and communicate results through professional cartographic layouts.

## Limitations

- This is an exploratory index-based analysis rather than a fully validated land-cover classification.
- In arid environments such as Dubai, NDBI can sometimes confuse bare soil, sand, and built-up surfaces.
- NDVI can be influenced by seasonal timing, irrigation, atmospheric conditions, and image acquisition differences.
